2019年4月｜OneK营地建设启动


2019年4月｜OneK营地正式开始建设。

对于许多位于野外的设施而言，营地往往只是进入自然的落脚点。然而从项目启动之初，我们便希望OneK能够有所不同。

我们并不希望在森林中建造一个与自然割裂的人类空间，而是希望建设一个能够与雨林长期共存的保护基地。

因此，从规划阶段开始，OneK便将基础设施本身视为生态保护的一部分。

为保护而设计

OneK的建设目标并不仅仅是提供住宿和工作空间。

更重要的是探索一种能够在热带雨林环境中长期运行、同时尽可能减少生态影响的建设模式。

在整个建设过程中，我们始终围绕一个核心原则：

减少生态扰动（Ecological Disturbance）。

这一理念贯穿于营地建设的各个方面：

	•	尽可能减少森林清理面积；
	•	保留原有大型树木和自然植被；
	•	控制施工区域范围；
	•	降低对土壤和水体的影响；
	•	避免不必要的生态破碎化。

基础设施也是保护工作的一部分

随着营地逐步建成，我们开始建立一套更加符合保护区需求的管理体系。

从能源利用到供水系统，从污水处理到垃圾管理，每一个环节都以降低环境影响为目标。

我们逐渐形成了一套属于OneK自己的原则：

	•	优先使用可持续能源方案；
	•	生活污水全部进入地下处理系统；
	•	垃圾分类并运出保护区处理；
	•	减少一次性塑料使用；
	•	控制夜间光污染和噪音干扰；
	•	道路与设施维护尽量减少对周边生态系统影响。

我们相信，真正的生态保护不仅发生在森林之中，也体现在人类如何建设和管理自己的活动空间。

从营地到生物站

最初的OneK营地规模并不大。

它只是一个位于雨林中的工作基地。

然而随着时间推移，这里逐渐成为巡护工作的后勤中心、生物多样性调查的出发点、长期生态监测的平台，以及后来OneK Biological Station的雏形。

许多后来开展的项目——鸟类调查、昆虫监测、红外相机研究、河流生态恢复以及环境教育活动——都从这里开始。

一种与森林共存的尝试

对于OneK而言，营地建设从来不仅仅是一项工程。

它更像是一场实验。

一场关于人类如何在热带雨林中生活、工作，同时尽可能减少对自然影响的长期实验。

这项实验至今仍在继续。

April 2019｜Construction of the OneK Field Camp Begins

In April 2019, construction officially began on the OneK field camp.

For many projects located in remote natural areas, a field camp serves simply as a place to stay and work. From the very beginning, however, we envisioned something different for OneK.

Rather than creating a human space separated from the surrounding rainforest, we sought to establish a conservation base that could coexist with nature over the long term.

As a result, infrastructure was never viewed as separate from conservation. Instead, it became an integral part of our conservation philosophy.

Designed for Conservation

The goal of the OneK camp was not simply to provide accommodation and operational facilities.

Our objective was to explore a model for living and working within a tropical rainforest while minimizing environmental impacts and supporting long-term conservation efforts.

From the planning stage onward, every decision was guided by a single principle:

Minimizing Ecological Disturbance.

This principle influenced every aspect of construction:

	•	Limiting forest clearing to the smallest area possible;
	•	Preserving existing mature trees and native vegetation;
	•	Reducing soil disturbance during construction;
	•	Protecting nearby streams and natural drainage systems;
	•	Avoiding unnecessary habitat fragmentation.

Infrastructure as Part of Conservation

As the camp gradually took shape, we developed management practices designed to reduce the ecological footprint of daily operations.

From energy systems and water supply to wastewater treatment, waste management, and road maintenance, every element was planned with environmental responsibility in mind.

Over time, several guiding principles emerged:

	•	Prioritize sustainable and renewable energy solutions;
	•	Treat wastewater through underground treatment systems;
	•	Remove and properly manage waste outside the reserve;
	•	Minimize the use of single-use plastics;
	•	Reduce light and noise pollution;
	•	Maintain roads and facilities with minimal impact on surrounding habitats.

We believe that conservation does not occur only in the forest itself—it is also reflected in how people design, build, and manage the spaces they occupy.

From Field Camp to Biological Station

The original OneK camp was modest in size.

It began as a simple operational base in the rainforest.

Over time, however, it evolved into the logistical center for conservation patrols, biodiversity surveys, ecological monitoring, and environmental education activities. Eventually, it became the foundation of what is now the OneK Biological Station.

Many of the projects that followed—including bird monitoring, insect surveys, camera trap studies, stream restoration efforts, and long-term ecological research—started here.

An Ongoing Experiment in Coexistence

For OneK, the camp has never been just a collection of buildings.

It represents an ongoing effort to answer a larger question:

How can people live, work, and contribute to conservation while minimizing their impact on a tropical rainforest ecosystem?

That experiment continues today.
